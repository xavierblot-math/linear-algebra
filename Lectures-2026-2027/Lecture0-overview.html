Who is teaching this course

Xavier Blot

I am a researcher at the CMLS, the mathematics department here at École polytechnique.

I work in enumerative geometry: the art of counting geometric objects (curves passing through given points, coverings of a surface, and so on).

These counts show up in physics, in particular in string theory. More surprisingly, they satisfy equations describing the propagation of certain waves, which connects them to mathematical physics. Understanding why is a large part of what I do.

Exercise sheets

Two kinds of exercises

Fundamental

Do most of them.

These are the baseline. To practice the core notions. This is where you should focus most of the effort, and time.

Advanced

Pick a few. Take your time.

This is a challenge. Do not try to cover them all. Choose one or two and sit with them properly. An hour on one good problem beats ten minutes on six of them. It is okay if you do not manage to solve it properly.

To develop logical and abstract thinking in more advanced settings.

How to work

Being stuck is part of learning

Understanding a solution is not the same as being able to produce one. Watching a clean proof feels like learning: every line follows, nothing is confusing. But doing it alone, later, in front of a blank page, is another story.

The effort is the mechanism. Recovering a definition from memory, choosing which theorem applies, writing a proof from nothing. This is where the learning actually happens. (active recall and production).

So twenty minutes stuck are not wasted. They also change how you read the solution: you know exactly which step you were missing, and that is the step you will remember.

A concrete recipe
  • Try alone, 15–20 minutes, before any help.
  • Then ask a TA, a classmate, or read the solution.
  • Redo it from scratch a day later, notes closed. This step is the one everyone skips and the one that works.
Solutions & AI

Where AI helps, and where it costs you

Reasonable
  • Asking for more detail on one step you could not follow.
  • Asking for a second explanation of a definition, or an example.
  • Checking a computation you have already done yourself.

Used this way, after you have struggled, it is a good tutor: infinitely patient, available at midnight.

A waste of your own time
  • Pasting the exercise in and copying the answer.

You end up with a correct page and nothing else. It feels like you will remember it. You will not.

Rule: use it after being stuck, never instead of being stuck. Solutions are posted at the end of the day anyway.
