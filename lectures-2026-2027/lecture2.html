Lecture 2 : vector subspaces and span of a family of vectors

Vector subspaces

Example in \(\mathbb{K}^3\)

Recall \(\mathbb{K}^3=\{(x,y,z)\mid x,y,z\in\mathbb{K}\}\). Consider \[ \mathbb{K}^2\times\{0\}=\{(x,y,0)\in\mathbb{K}^3\} \;=\;\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{K}^3\mid z=0\}. \] The vector-space operations of \(\mathbb{K}^3\) preserve this set:

  • \((0,0,0)\in \mathbb{K}^2\times\{0\}\).
  • \((x,y,0)+(x',y',0)=(x+x',y+y',0)\in \mathbb{K}^2\times\{0\}\).
  • \(\lambda\cdot(x,y,0)=(\lambda x,\lambda y,0)\in \mathbb{K}^2\times\{0\}\).

Hence the vector-space structure on \(\mathbb{K}^3\) induces one on \(\mathbb{K}^2\times\{0\}\) (the \(xy\)-plane).

Definition (vector subspace)

Let \(E\) be a \(\mathbb{K}\)-vector space and \(F\subset E\). We say that \(F\) is a vector subspace of \(E\) if:

  1. \(0_E\in F\).
  2. \(F\) is closed under addition: \(x,y\in F\Rightarrow x+y\in F\).
  3. \(F\) is closed under scalar multiplication: \(\lambda\in\mathbb{K},\,x\in F\Rightarrow \lambda x\in F\).

Variant (equivalent):

  1. \(F\neq\varnothing\).
  2. \(\forall x,y\in F,\ \forall \lambda\in\mathbb{K},\ \lambda x + y\in F\).

The \(\mathbb{K}\)-vector space structure of \(E\) induces a \(\mathbb{K}\)-vector space structure on \(F\), namely:

Lemma

Let \(F\) be a vector subspace of a \(\mathbb{K}\)-vector space \(E\). Then the operations of \(E\) restrict to \(F\), and \(F\) equipped with them is a \(\mathbb{K}\)-vector space.

Proof. By (b) and (c), the addition and the scalar multiplication of \(E\) restrict to maps \(F\times F\to F\) and \(\mathbb{K}\times F\to F\). By (a) we have \(0_E\in F\), and it is a neutral element for the addition of \(F\) since it is one for the addition of \(E\). If \(x\in F\), then \(-x=(-1)\cdot x\in F\) by (c), so every element of \(F\) has an opposite in \(F\). The remaining axioms — associativity and commutativity of the addition, the two distributivities, \((\lambda\mu)x=\lambda(\mu x)\) and \(1\cdot x=x\) — are identities, valid for all elements of \(E\) and hence in particular for those of \(F\): there is nothing to check.

In practice, one almost never verifies the axioms directly: to prove that a set is a \(\mathbb{K}\)-vector space, one realises it as a vector subspace of a space already known to be one.

Examples/non-examples
  1. \(\varnothing\) is never a vector subspace.
  2. \(\{0\}\) is always a vector subspace.
  3. \(E\) itself is a vector subspace of \(E\).
  4. As \(\mathbb{Q}\)-vector spaces: \(\mathbb{Q}\subset \mathbb{R}\subset \mathbb{C}\) (each inclusion is a \(\mathbb{Q}\)-subspace).
  5. \(\mathbb{K}[X]\) (polynomials over \(\mathbb{K}\)) is a \(\mathbb{K}\)-vector space by componentwise operations; the set \(\mathbb{K}[X]_{\le d}=\{a_0+a_1X+\dots+a_dX^d\mid a_i\in\mathbb{K}\}\) is a vector subspace of \(\mathbb{K}[X]\).
  6. \(F=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{K}^2\mid xy=0\}=\mathbb{K}\times\{0\}\ \cup\ \{0\}\times\mathbb{K}\) is not a subspace of \(\mathbb{K}^2\) (union of axes, not stable under addition).

In the picture, \( (1,1)\) is not in the union of the axes.

Span of a family of vectors

Definition (linear combination)

Let \(v_1,\dots,v_m\) be vectors in a \(\mathbb{K}\)-vector space \(E\). A linear combination of \(v_1,\dots,v_m\) is a vector of the form

\[ \lambda_1 v_1+\cdots+\lambda_m v_m, \]

where $\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_m\in\mathbb{K}.$

Definition (span)

The span of \(v_1,\dots,v_m\) is the set of all their linear combinations:

\[ \langle v_1,\dots,v_m\rangle=\operatorname{Span}(v_1,\dots,v_m) =\{\lambda_1 v_1+\cdots+\lambda_m v_m \mid \lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_m\in\mathbb{K}\}. \]

More generally, for any subset \(A\subset E\) (possibly infinite), \(\operatorname{Span}(A)\) is the set of linear combinations of finitely many elements of \(A\):

\[ \operatorname{Span}(A)=\{\lambda_1 a_1+\cdots+\lambda_m a_m \mid m\geq 1,\ a_1,\dots,a_m\in A,\ \lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_m\in\mathbb{K}\}. \]
Convention

For the empty family \(\varnothing\), we set \(\operatorname{Span}(\varnothing)=\{0\}\). This is consistent with the convention that an empty sum is equal to \(0\).

Remarks
  • The word comes from the verb to span, i.e. to cover: the vectors \(v_1,\dots,v_m\) “cover” the set \(\operatorname{Span}(v_1,\dots,v_m)\).
  • You may also meet the notation \(\operatorname{Vect}(v_1,\dots,v_m)\) (French notation, from sous-espace vectoriel engendré). In these notes we write \(\operatorname{Span}\).
Lines and planes in \(\mathbb{R}^3\)
  • Let \(u\in\mathbb{R}^3\), \(u\neq 0\). Then \(\operatorname{Span}(u)=\{\lambda u\mid \lambda\in\mathbb{R}\}\) is the line passing through \(0\) directed by \(u\).
  • Let \(u,v\in\mathbb{R}^3\), \(u,v\neq 0\). Then \[ \operatorname{Span}(u,v)= \begin{cases} \text{a plane} & \text{if } u,v \text{ are not collinear},\\ \text{a line} & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases} \] Indeed, if \(v=\lambda u\) for some \(\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\), then \(\operatorname{Span}(u,\lambda u)=\operatorname{Span}(u)\).

The whole space \(\mathbb{K}^3\)

We have \[ \operatorname{Span}\left(\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\0\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\\0\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}0\\0\\1\end{pmatrix}\right)=\mathbb{K}^3. \] Indeed, this span is the set of vectors \[ \lambda_1\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\0\end{pmatrix}+\lambda_2\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\\0\end{pmatrix}+\lambda_3\begin{pmatrix}0\\0\\1\end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_1\\\lambda_2\\\lambda_3\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3\in\mathbb{K}, \] which is exactly \(\mathbb{K}^3\).

A redundant family in \(\mathbb{K}^2\)

\[ \operatorname{Span}\left(\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\end{pmatrix}\right) =\operatorname{Span}\left(\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\end{pmatrix}\right)=\mathbb{K}^2. \] The third vector brings nothing new, since \(\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\end{pmatrix}\).

A plane in \(\mathbb{K}^3\) and its equation

The span \(\operatorname{Span}\left(\begin{pmatrix}1\\2\\0\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\\1\end{pmatrix}\right)\) is a plane in \(\mathbb{K}^3\). Its elements are the vectors \[ \lambda_1\begin{pmatrix}1\\2\\0\end{pmatrix}+\lambda_2\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\\1\end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_1\\2\lambda_1+\lambda_2\\\lambda_2\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \lambda_1,\lambda_2\in\mathbb{K}. \] So a vector \((x,y,z)\) of the span satisfies \[ \begin{cases} x=\lambda_1,\\ y=2\lambda_1+\lambda_2,\\ z=\lambda_2, \end{cases} \] and eliminating \(\lambda_1,\lambda_2\) gives the equation of the plane: \[ \boxed{\,2x-y+z=0\,} \] Conversely, if \(2x-y+z=0\), take \(\lambda_1=x\) and \(\lambda_2=z\): then \(y=2\lambda_1+\lambda_2\), so \((x,y,z)\) lies in the span. Hence \[ \operatorname{Span}\left(\begin{pmatrix}1\\2\\0\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\\1\end{pmatrix}\right)=\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{K}^3\mid 2x-y+z=0\}. \]

Polynomials
  • \(\operatorname{Span}(1,X,X^2,\dots,X^d)=\mathbb{K}[X]_{\le d}\), the space of polynomials of degree \(\le d\).
  • \(\operatorname{Span}(\{1,X,X^2,\dots\})=\mathbb{K}[X]\). Here the family is infinite, but every polynomial is a linear combination of finitely many monomials \(X^k\).
Lemma (the span is a subspace)

Let \(E\) be a \(\mathbb{K}\)-vector space and \(A\subset E\) a subset.

  1. \(\operatorname{Span}(A)\) is a vector subspace of \(E\), and \(v\in\operatorname{Span}(A)\) for all \(v\in A\).
  2. If \(H\subset E\) is a vector subspace and \(A\subseteq H\), then \(\operatorname{Span}(A)\subseteq H\).

Proof. Left as an exercise.

Remark

In other words, \(\operatorname{Span}(A)\) is the smallest vector subspace of \(E\) containing \(A\).
